## China (2024 Article IV) vs. United States (2024 Article IV): Fiscal Recommendations

### 1. China — reorient spending, reduce precautionary saving

## 2. Assessment against the 2024 Article IV report

**The report's condition for cutting rates.** The 2024 Article IV (IMF Country Report, PR24/281, physical PDF page 2) describes disinflation as having "taken a relatively light toll," notes PCE inflation of 2.7 percent in April (down from a 7.1 percent peak in 2022), and states that inflation "is expected to return to 2 percent by mid-2025." The operative condition is therefore convergence of inflation to the FOMC's 2 percent goal — the report frames the 525 bps of tightening as having "helped guide inflation back toward the FOMC's 2 percent goal," i.e., easing is conditioned on inflation durably returning to target, not on the level of unemployment or on a multi-year average of past inflation. (The excerpt does not contain an explicit sentence of the form "the Fed should cut when X"; the condition above is inferred from the stated 2 percent goal and the mid-2025 return projection. A more explicit policy-path recommendation would need to be located in the staff report body, which is not in the retrieved excerpts.)

**Do the two statistics alone justify an immediate cut? No.**
- The 5.61% figure is a backward-looking average of 2021–2023 annual-average CPI growth. It is above 2 percent, so on its own it argues *against* easing; it says nothing about the current stance of policy or the inflation outlook.
- The −4.42 pp unemployment change is a comparison of 2023 with the pandemic-distorted 2020 level. It shows labor-market normalization since the 2020 shock, not a signal of slack requiring stimulus. The report's own framing is that activity and employment "now exceed pre-pandemic expectations" (p. 2), i.e., the labor market is tight rather than weak.
- Neither statistic measures the report's stated condition (inflation returning to 2 percent). The report's projection is that this happens by mid-2025, which is a forecast, not an observation, and it is not established by the two WDI statistics.

**Distinguishing the inflation-target discussion from the annual CPI statistic.**
- The report's 2 percent figure is the FOMC's target, measured against **PCE inflation** (headline and core), and discussed in terms of a projected path (2.7% in April 2024; return to 2% by mid-2025). This is a forward-looking policy target on a different price index.
- The 5.61% calculated here is **CPI** annual-average growth (FP.CPI.TOTL.ZG) for calendar years 2021–2023 — a retrospective, index-specific, three-year simple mean. CPI and PCE differ in coverage and weighting, and an annual average differs from a point-in-time or 12-month rate. The two numbers are therefore not directly comparable, and the 5.61% should not be read as "inflation is 5.61% relative to a 2% target."

**Caveats.** The retrieved excerpts are from the 2023 and 2024 Article IV documents; the 2024 excerpt (p. 2) is the Executive Board press release and Board assessment, which is qualitative. No excerpt states an explicit rate-cut recommendation, so any claim that the report recommends an immediate cut (or its timing) is not supported by the retrieved evidence.
