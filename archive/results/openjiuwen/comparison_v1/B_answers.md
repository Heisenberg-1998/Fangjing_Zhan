# China vs. United States: Fiscal Recommendations in the 2024 Article IV Reports

## China (IMF 2024 Article IV, People's Republic of China)

**Recommendation — budget-neutral reallocation, not aggregate consolidation in 2024.** The IMF recommends "a budget-neutral fiscal package, which includes a reorientation of expenditures toward households" to support growth during the property-sector adjustment and local-government (LG) fiscal reforms (CHN_2024_article_iv, physical PDF p. 34, ¶43; repeated at p. 49, ¶82). The package is explicitly *deficit-neutral*: higher social spending financed by lower off-budget investment and higher tax revenue, so it supports growth "without increasing debt risks" (p. 34, ¶43; p. 49, ¶82). This is a **reallocation within a roughly unchanged overall balance**, distinct from consolidation.

**Aggregate consolidation is a medium-term recommendation.** "Over the medium term, sustained fiscal consolidation driven by lower off-budget investment and higher tax revenues is needed to stabilize the general government debt-to-GDP ratio" (p. 49, ¶82). So the report separates: near-term budget-neutral recomposition (2024) from medium-term consolidation.

**How to reorient spending and reduce precautionary saving.** Social protection should be "structurally strengthened by raising the adequacy of non-urban pensions, unemployment benefits, social assistance, and expanding medical benefits, which would reduce the need for precautionary savings" (p. 34, ¶43; p. 49, ¶82). Revenue could come from capital taxation (higher rates, fewer deductions) (p. 34, ¶43). Unintended tightening from LG financing constraints should be offset with temporary central-government transfers (p. 34, ¶43). The 2023 report's Box 2 gives the underlying diagnosis — gaps in social protection lead households to self-insure through high precautionary savings — but that is a *2023* document (CHN_2023_article_iv, p. 37, Box 2), not the 2024 report; treat it as background, not a 2024 recommendation.

**Why fiscal adjustment is needed.** The deficit and debt are "projected to remain well above pre-pandemic forecasts"; the debt-GDP ratio is expected to exceed 140 percent by 2032, and chronic deficits stem largely from health and social-security outlays amid population aging — "a significant and persistent policy misalignment that needs to be urgently addressed" (USA_2024_article_iv, p. 24, ¶¶18–19).
